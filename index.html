Purpose

You can’t build a future you can’t imagine.

That’s the whole bet of solarpunk.

1

Break dystopia fatigue

Give climate imagination somewhere to go besides collapse.

2

Make alternatives concrete

Stories and images as rehearsals for real policy, design and neighborhoods.

3

Keep the punk

Hope that’s practical and political: build it yourself, decentralize, share.

Already here

Where it already exists

None of these call themselves solarpunk, and that’s the point. Tap a city to see it.

Community solarNeighbors co-own panels and share the power and the profits.
Urban growingRooftop farms, food forests, seed libraries, guerrilla gardens.
Repair cultureRepair cafés and right-to-repair laws keep things out of landfill.
Open hardwareShared designs for tools, tractors and microgrids anyone can build.
Green architecturePlant-wrapped buildings like Milan’s Bosco Verticale.
Walkable townsBikes, trains and 15-minute neighborhoods over car-first sprawl.
